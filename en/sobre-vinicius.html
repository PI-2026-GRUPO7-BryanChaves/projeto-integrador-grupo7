About Me

Vinicius Francisco Caramel

Birth

On August 26, 2011, my son was born. It was a very important moment for our whole family. Almost all his relatives were there, including my mother, who came from Tapejara to see her grandson. It was definitely a special day.

School Journey

My school journey had many changes. I started school when I was four years old at Borges de Medeiros Municipal School. I stayed there until I entered middle school, when I moved to Vereador Francisco Gualdino de Lima State School. I studied there until the ninth grade. When I started high school, I tried to get into the Federal Institute of Paraná (IFPR), where I currently study.

Historical Event

My birthday is celebrated on August 26, a date that is very special to me and also has great historical importance. On this day, in 1789, the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen was approved during the French Revolution, defending values such as freedom, equality, and justice. In 1920, the United States ratified the amendment that guaranteed women the right to vote. I find it interesting that I was born on a date marked by such important events, representing achievements, changes, and rights. So, August 26 is not special only because it is my birthday, but also because it is part of world history.

Current Moment

As I enter high school, I realize that things are getting harder and that I can no longer just let things happen and hope everything works out. I am gaining more responsibilities and have big plans for the future.

Future Plans

Honestly, I am not sure about my future yet, but I intend to study subjects related to mathematics and science. One day, I might become an engineer or work in a similar field. My expectations are moderate, so I hope to achieve whatever goals are necessary.

Where We Live

Vinicius Francisco Caramel

The Municipality of Toledo

I live in Toledo, in the western region of Paraná. Our city began to be settled in 1946 and was officially established in 1952. Toledo is known for agriculture, pig farming, and industry. One of its most famous events is the Pig on a Spit Festival. Here we have beautiful places, such as the Municipal Lake, the Aquarium, and Christ the King Cathedral. Our city has strong Italian and German cultural influences. One interesting fact is that Toledo is known as the Capital of Pig on a Spit. I like living in Toledo because it is a beautiful, welcoming city and an important place in Paraná.

Jardim Gisela Neighborhood

I live in Jardim Gisela, a neighborhood in Toledo with many houses, stores, schools, and recreational areas. It is a neighborhood where many families live and spend time together. One well-known place is the Arroio Marreco Linear Park, where people can walk, ride bicycles, and enjoy nature. We also have squares and a public restaurant. One interesting fact is that the neighborhood has a farmers' market where fresh food is sold. I like living in Jardim Gisela because it is an important part of our city and of my daily life.

Short Story: The Boy on the Bus

By Bryan Douglas Machado Chaves

Mr. Osvaldo has been driving the bus for 22 years and swears he knows all of Toledo just by listening to the passengers. But that October morning, something was different.

Every day, at almost seven o'clock, an eleven-year-old boy got on the bus, carrying a backpack bigger than himself, with some wires sticking out. He sat at the front, right behind the driver, playing with a little board, with his tongue sticking out in concentration.

— What is that, young man?

— A robot, Mr. Osvaldo. It doesn't walk yet. But it will.

— And who taught you how to do that?

— At school. In the morning, we study regular subjects, and in the afternoon, we go to the laboratory.

Mr. Osvaldo laughed, thinking it was just a child's exaggeration. But the boy was right: in 2026, 150 students from the state school system with high abilities attend Donaduzzi School, where they follow the regular curriculum in the morning and, in the afternoon, take part in research, robotics, and science activities. All of this happens inside Biopark, which brings together around 170 companies and has just celebrated its tenth anniversary. Mr. Osvaldo remembered that when he started driving that route, the area was nothing but bushes and dirt roads. Nobody talked about laboratories, much less robots.

At the last stop, the boy got off in a hurry, his backpack bouncing on his back.

— Bye, Mr. Osvaldo! Next week I'll bring it walking!

The driver watched the boy until he disappeared from sight. Then he adjusted the rearview mirror, smiled slightly, and thought, almost without realizing it: "I've been taking people into the future for 22 years, and I didn't even know it."

He put the bus into first gear and returned to the starting point. There were more people waiting.
