About Me

Bryan Douglas Machado Chaves

Birth

I remember September 8, 2011 as a very special day for our family. That was the day my son was born, at Hospital Bom Jesus, in Toledo, Paraná, at 8:45 in the morning. At that moment it was just the two of us, his parents, together with the doctors who were part of that important moment. After the birth, the initial calm did not last long. Little by little, a large number of relatives began to arrive at the hospital to meet the newest member of the family. The beginning of his life ended up marked by a lot of movement, visits and a certain disorder, since everyone wanted to be there and take part in the moment. Despite all the mess of those first days, I remember that time fondly. His birth brought a new phase to our family and, ever since, his presence has been part of our story.

School History

My school history began at the age of four, when I entered the Escola Municipal Carlos Friedrich. That was where I had my first contact with the school environment and began to build my first experiences as a student. In those early years I also had some experiences outside the classroom that stayed with me, especially the visits to the zoo, which were fun moments, different from the school routine. The 4th and 5th grades were different because of the pandemic. The changes caused by isolation and remote learning got in the way of part of the school experience I would normally have had. After that period I moved on to the Colégio Estadual Novo Horizonte, which was the state school in my neighborhood and was also a little closer to my home. The change was a fairly natural step and, even though the school later became a civic-military school, my adaptation was relatively smooth. I find it fairly easy to follow rules, which helped in the process. It was also at the Colégio Estadual Novo Horizonte that I met friends who stayed by my side for a good part of my school years, up to the 9th grade. Those friendships became an important part of that stage. After finishing Middle School, I decided to look for a change and for an opportunity that I considered better for my education. That is how I came to the Federal Institute of Paraná (IFPR). Today I find this new stage quite fun. The institution has good facilities and, above all, I have met interesting people who made the experience even more meaningful. Today I see my school history as a sequence of changes that gradually led me to an environment that I consider right for continuing to learn and develop.

Historical Fact: Brazil's Independence

One of the most important historical events related to my date of birth happened exactly one day before I was born. On September 7, 1822, Dom Pedro declared the Independence of Brazil on the banks of the Ipiranga stream, in São Paulo, politically breaking Brazil's ties with Portugal. Independence, however, did not happen suddenly. Brazil had been going through political changes since the arrival of the Portuguese royal family in 1808, and later because of the pressure from the Portuguese Cortes for Dom Pedro to return to Portugal. His decision to stay in Brazil, known as the Dia do Fico ("Day of Staying"), contributed to the process that would end in the declaration of independence. It is also important to understand that September 7 was mainly the symbolic landmark of Independence. The political separation was not immediately accepted in the entire Brazilian territory, and conflicts continued in some regions, such as Bahia, where the fight for independence went on until 1823. So there is a curious coincidence between my story and this event: while Brazil's Independence is celebrated on September 7, I was born the next day, September 8. In this way, one of the main landmarks of the country's history is right next to my date of birth.

Current Moment

Right now I am going through a phase of change and greater responsibility in my school life. Studying at IFPR brought a higher level of demand than I was used to in my previous schools. This change took me a little out of my comfort zone and made me realize that I could not depend only on what I learn in class. For that reason I started to build a study routine and I try to keep it fairly steady, even if on some days the number of tasks makes the routine heavier. Among my subjects, I am most interested in Mathematics, Chemistry, LAEC, Programming Logic, Philosophy and History. I also have some difficulties, mainly in Physics and Sociology, but I notice that when I spend more time studying, I understand these subjects better. This has shown me that having difficulty with a subject does not necessarily mean I cannot learn it, but that I need to find the time and the right way to study it. Socially, I get along well with most people, although I have my own group of friends. Besides classes, IFPR also offers opportunities that I did not find in the same way in my previous schools, such as scientific initiation projects. I currently take part in a project related to fish, called FISH-INOS, which has been a different experience and an opportunity to get to know scientific research better. So I consider my current moment a phase of adaptation, learning and growth. I am still learning to deal with a more demanding routine, but I am also discovering areas I am interested in and developing more autonomy in my studies.

Future Prospects

When I think about my future, I feel that I can still go further than I have gone so far. I believe I have the potential to achieve bigger results, but I also recognize that this depends mainly on the effort I am willing to put into it. Leaving my comfort zone is one of my greatest difficulties, precisely because it requires discipline and consistency, but I know that developing these qualities will be important to reach my goals. Right now, my main intention is to study Computer Science, a field that brings together some of the subjects I like most, especially programming, logic and mathematics. At the same time, I do not want to limit my possibilities. I am also interested in following fields such as Physics, Mathematics or Chemistry, mainly because of the chance to work with more concrete, scientific knowledge. During High School, I intend to make the most of the opportunities that come up. I want to take part in several academic olympiads, projects related to my interests and scientific initiation experiences. I also plan to take the PAS of some universities and, out of curiosity and as a personal challenge, to try the ITA admission process. I still do not know exactly which path I will follow in the future, and I consider that a good thing. I prefer to explore different areas before making a final decision. If my main plans do not work out, I also consider becoming a teacher of Mathematics, Physics or Chemistry. Whatever path I choose, I hope to keep studying, discovering new areas and making the most of my potential to build a path I can be proud of.

Where I Live

Toledo, Paraná

The Municipality of Toledo

I was born and I live in Toledo, a city in the western region of the state of Paraná. The name of the municipality comes from the Toledo River, which crosses its territory, and people born here are called "toledanos" or "toledenses". Toledo's history is recent compared to that of other Brazilian cities. The region began to be settled in 1946, in a colonization process organized by the Industrial Madeireira Colonizadora Rio Paraná S.A., known as Maripá. The company divided and sold plots of land to settlers, largely descendants of Germans and Italians who came from Rio Grande do Sul and Santa Catarina, and founded villages around the main town. In 1952, Toledo became a municipality. As it grew, parts of its territory gave rise to other cities in the region, such as Assis Chateaubriand, Nova Santa Rosa, Ouro Verde do Oeste and São Pedro do Iguaçu, and also contributed to the formation of Marechal Cândido Rondon. Today, Toledo's economy is based mainly on farming and livestock, agroindustry, and small and medium-sized industry. According to a study published by Unioeste using IBGE data, the municipality had about 144 thousand inhabitants in 2021 and an HDI of 0.768. It is worth remembering that Toledo's official history tends to highlight the figure of the pioneers, but researchers also show that there are many other memories of how the city was formed, lived by the settlers and workers themselves.

The Neighborhood: Jardim Coopagro

I live in Jardim Coopagro, a neighborhood in the north zone of Toledo. According to a study by the Faculdade Assis Gurgacz (FAG), it has about 8.4 thousand inhabitants, which places it among the most populous neighborhoods in the city. The name of the neighborhood is tied to that of Coopagro, an agroindustrial cooperative that was part of the region's history and was even the subject of a master's thesis in History at Unioeste. Today, the neighborhood brings together homes, shops and services linked to agribusiness, such as the Toledo Rural Union and financial branches focused on the sector, on Avenida Ministro Cirne Lima, one of its main streets. The Faculdade Assis Gurgacz is also on Avenida Ministro Cirne Lima. For me, what most marks Jardim Coopagro is the presence of its schools: it was here that I studied at the Escola Municipal Carlos Friedrich and at the Colégio Estadual Novo Horizonte, and it is here that most of my childhood took place.

TCTs

Thematic Macro-Area: Science and Technology

The Cross-Cutting Contemporary Themes (TCTs) of the BNCC, Brazil's national curriculum framework, gather subjects that are part of people's lives and that cut across several school subjects. The macro-area drawn for our group was Science and Technology, which deals with scientific development and innovation. In Toledo this theme shows up in a concrete way, because the city is home to a technology park, universities and projects that connect research to the development of the region.

Actions in Toledo

  • Biopark: the Science and Technology Park of Biosciences, in Toledo, covers more than five million square meters and is focused on research and innovation. The project grew out of the pharmaceutical company Prati-Donaduzzi and brings together universities such as UFPR (with its Medicine program), UTFPR, IFPR and Unioeste, as well as the Donaduzzi College and School and dozens of companies.
  • Embrapa at Biopark: an Embrapa unit dedicated to research on poultry, pigs and fish was set up at Biopark, which links science to the region's main economic activity, agribusiness.
  • UTFPR Toledo Campus: the university offers programs such as Computer Engineering and Internet Systems, runs technological extension projects and maintains the professional master's degree in Biosciences Technologies, which operates inside Biopark.
  • Usina do Conhecimento ("Knowledge Factory"): UTFPR and the City Hall of Toledo signed a technical cooperation agreement to manage this education and innovation space, located on the peninsula of the Diva Paim Ecological Park, with four areas: Science, Lab, Mind and Work, aimed at fields such as biology, physics and mathematics.

Chronicle: The Boy on the Bus

By Bryan Douglas Machado Chaves

Mr. Osvaldo has been driving the bus for 22 years and swears he knows all of Toledo just by the sound of the passengers. But that October morning, something was different.

Every day, around 7 o'clock, an eleven-year-old boy got on, with a backpack bigger than he was and some wires sticking out of it. He sat at the front, right behind the driver, and kept tinkering with a little board, tongue sticking out.

"What's that you've got there, son?"

"A robot, Mr. Osvaldo. It doesn't walk yet. But it will."

"And who taught you that?"

"School. In the morning we study like normal, in the afternoon we go to the lab."

Mr. Osvaldo laughed, thinking it was just a kid exaggerating. But the boy was right: in 2026, 150 state-school students with high abilities attend the Colégio Donaduzzi, where they follow the regular curriculum in the morning and, in the afternoon shift, do research, robotics and science. All of this inside Biopark, which brings together about 170 companies and has just turned ten. Mr. Osvaldo remembered that, when he started on that route, there was nothing there but brush and dirt road. Nobody talked about labs, much less robots.

At the last stop, the boy got off in a hurry, his backpack bouncing on his back.

"Bye, Mr. Osvaldo! Next week I'll bring it walking!"

The driver kept watching the kid until he disappeared from sight. Then he adjusted the rearview mirror, gave a little half smile and thought, almost without meaning to: "22 years taking people to the future and I never even knew it."

He put it in first gear and headed back to the first stop. There were more people waiting.
